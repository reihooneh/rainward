What it does

Follows every drop

The lot becomes a grid of half-metre cells. A priority flood finds every hollow and where it overflows; water soaks in at your soil's rate, fills hollows and spills downhill, split among lower neighbours by slope.

Watches the storm

A shallow-water simulation, the kind flood engineers use, plays the storm out second by second, so you can watch sheets of water cross the lawn and pools rise and drain. Every litre is accounted for.

Plans for the money

It tries rain gardens in the flow paths, downpipe extensions, pipes into gardens, tanks and regrading, alone and together, and picks what keeps the most water off the house and the neighbours within your budget.

Why it matters

Water against a house's footings is a leading cause of damp, cracked slabs and subsiding paths, and the usual fixes are cheap if they're in the right place. A rain garden in the wrong spot catches nothing; a downpipe extension pointing uphill just brings the water back.

Councils also ask homes not to concentrate runoff onto the neighbours or overload street drains. Rainward shows exactly where each litre goes, so you can see which change does what before anyone picks up a shovel.

Built to be trusted

  • The engine is Go, compiled to WebAssembly, running on your device.
  • Tested against known answers: a bowl's exact volume, every drop accounted for, the two water models agreeing, and the planner matching exhaustive search on small lots.
  • No sign-in, no tracking, nothing sent anywhere.
